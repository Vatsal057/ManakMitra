# ManakMitra Dataset Enrichment Report

Covers three rounds of work: round 1 (normalization, archive.org scraping,
allied-standard ingestion), round 2 (tri-state certification, review
severity, derived versions, category fixes, hand-verified QCOs), and round
3 (wiring the frontend to this contract, fixing a scope-severity bug round
2 shipped, landing-page statistics honesty). Numbers below are from the
current `data/bis_standards_clean.csv` / `.json`, generated by
`src/data_pipeline/clean.py`, and are reproducible by running
`python scripts/validate_dataset.py`.

**Read this alongside the two prompts** in `prompts/`. This report doesn't
re-explain what was asked; it records what actually happened, what's still
wrong, and exactly what to check before anyone presents this in a demo.

**Whole-corpus vs. demo-spine.** The 45 primary standards in
`allied_standards_mapping_normalized.json` are what the demo actually
displays (a search result plus its "Allied Standards" expansion). Every
number below is reported both ways because they diverge a lot — the
corpus-wide numbers are the honest figure for a deck about data quality;
the demo-spine numbers are what determines whether a live demo embarrasses
anyone.

## 2. What was scraped vs. drafted vs. derived

| Field | How it's populated | Coverage (whole corpus) | Coverage (demo spine, n=45) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `scope_description` | **scraped** from archive.org OCR (`_djvu.txt`, Clause 1 "Scope") where a scan was found; else **title_fallback** (the pre-existing duplicate-of-title text) | scraped: 240/287, title_fallback: 47/287 | scraped: 39/45, title_fallback: 6/45 |
| `year_published` | original scrape, corrected/backfilled from archive.org scan identifiers where missing or impossible | 280/287 have a year | 45/45 have a year |
| `category` | original scrape category where already meaningful; else keyword-classified (`confidence: medium`); 4 pre-existing wrong categories manually corrected (see §5) | 285/287 categorized | 45/45 categorized |
| `latest_version` | **derived**, never scraped: `IS <number>[-<part>]:<year>` built from `year_published`. States the edition on record, not currency. | 280/287 (= wherever a year exists) | 45/45 |
| `certification_type` / `mandatory` / `qco_reference` | **hand-verified** for a short, cited list only (§4); everything else is `Not determined` | 10/287 determined | 9/45 determined |
| the 61 standards referenced only from `allied_standards_mapping.json` | **ingested** as first-class rows using the mapping file's own `title` field (not scraped, not invented) | 61 rows added | 0 (none are primaries) |

No field in this dataset was invented. Every non-null `scope_description`
that isn't a title duplicate came from an actual archive.org OCR page
(identifier recorded in `source_url`); every `qco_reference` cites a named
order and gazette number; everything not checked says so via
`certification_type: "Not determined"` rather than a fabricated `"None"`.

## 3. Spike results (why archive.org and not the BIS portal)

- **BIS e-sale portal (`standardsbis.bsbedge.com`)**: dead end. Confirmed
  by fetching a live product page — it's an ASP.NET storefront that shows
  only a title, price, and "Add to Cart"; no clause/scope text exists in
  the HTML at any depth. Not scraped.
- **archive.org**: works. BIS standard scans are uploaded under a
  consistent identifier convention, `gov.in.is.<number>[.<part>].<year>`,
  in the `publicsafetycode` collection, with OCR'd `_djvu.txt` full text.
  `src/data_pipeline/scrape/archive_org.py` searches by number, downloads
  and caches the OCR text, and regex-extracts the "1 SCOPE" clause —
  disambiguated from the table-of-contents line (which also matches "1
  Scope") by digit density, since a TOC line is mostly page numbers and
  the real clause is prose.
- Known scraper limitations: OCR quality varies (occasional garbled
  section headers, handled by a keyword-based cutoff); some standards
  have no scan on archive.org at all (47 misses, all honestly flagged
  `scope_source: title_fallback`); a handful of scans matched the wrong
  part when a standard has many parts sharing one base number.

## 4. QCO verification (hand-verified only, per instruction — no gazette scraper built)

Ten standards carry a real, citable `qco_reference`. Nothing else in the
dataset asserts a certification requirement. Confidence reflects how the
citation was obtained:

| Standard | Certification basis | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| IS 269, IS 455, IS 1489, IS 8112, IS 12269, IS 8041 (all cement grades) | **Cement (Quality Control) Order, 2003** — DPIIT S.O. 191(E), 17 Feb 2003. Confirmed against BIS's own CMD-1 circular (Ref CMD-1/12:1, 08 Dec 2006), which tables every mandatorily-certified standard against its parent QC order — a primary-adjacent official source, read directly (not a certification-consultancy summary). | high |
| IS 302-2-3 (electric irons) | Same BIS CMD-1 circular lists `IS 302 (Pt.2/Sec.3)` by exact part/section under the **Electrical Wires, Cables, Appliances... (Quality Control) Order, 2003** (DPIIT S.O. 189(E), 17 Feb 2003). Note: `IS 302` has dozens of unrelated parts (clocks, massage appliances, etc.) — only this exact part is certified; the classifier keys on `(is_number, part)`, not the bare number, specifically to avoid over-claiming the rest of IS 302. | high |
| IS 2925 (industrial safety helmets) | Same circular, but under a narrower mandate: **Coal Mines Regulations 1957, Reg. 157(4)** — DGMS Circular No. 22/1966, dated 23 Apr 1966. This is a mining-sector certification requirement, not a general-market QCO — recorded as such; don't read it as "every IS 2925 helmet everywhere requires this." | medium |
| IS 1786 (HYSD/TMT rebar) | **Steel and Steel Products (Quality Control) Order, 2024** — Ministry of Steel S.O. 574(E), 5 Feb 2024, Schedule 1 (IS 1786:2008). Order name/number/date corroborated across multiple independent BIS-certification sources, but the schedule itself wasn't read directly — hence medium, not high. | medium |
| IS 4151 (two-wheeler helmets) | **Helmet for riders of Two Wheeler Motor Vehicles (Quality Control) Order, 2020** — MoRTH S.O. 4252(E), 26 Nov 2020, effective 1 Jun 2021 (IS 4151:2015). Same corroboration caveat as above. | medium |

**Everything else — 277 of 287 rows, 36 of 45 demo-spine rows — is
`Not determined`.** This is not a claim that they're uncertified; it's an
honest statement that nobody has checked. The demo-spine products still
needing a real QCO check (highest priority — these are what a reviewer
will click on): IS 456 (RCC structures), IS 383 (aggregates), IS 1077
(bricks), IS 458 (precast concrete pipes), IS 4160 (switch sockets), IS
4250 (food mixers), IS 368 (immersion heaters), IS 3452-2 (toggle
switches) — the full list of 36 is in `bis_standards_clean.csv` filtered
on `certification_type == "Not determined"`.

## 5. Category fixes

- The 61 standards ingested from the allied mapping arrived
  `Uncategorized` in round 1 because they bypassed `category_rules.py`
  entirely. Round 2 ran them through an expanded classifier (added
  vocabulary for test-method / bend-tensile / code-of-practice titles,
  which this batch is heavily weighted toward). Result: 60 of 61 now
  categorized; only `IS 4905` ("Random Sampling and Randomization
  Procedures" — a generic statistical method with no real domain) stays
  `Uncategorized`, deliberately, rather than forced into a wrong bucket.
- Whole-corpus `Uncategorized` count: **2** (`IS 4905`, and `IS 26002`, a
  genuine — if unusual — BIS assurance-engagement standard with no fitting
  category in this scheme). Demo-spine: **0**.
- **Found by inspection, not asked for, fixed anyway**: four demo-spine
  standards carried a category from the *original* scrape that was
  outright wrong (not `Uncategorized`, so neither round's "fix
  Uncategorized rows" pass would have touched them):
  - `IS 2925` (Industrial Safety Helmets) was `Cement & Construction` → `Safety & PPE`
  - `IS 4151` (two-wheeler helmets) was `Cement & Construction` → `Safety & PPE`
  - `IS 13450-2-4` (cardiac defibrillators) was `Electrical` → `Medical & Healthcare`
  - `IS 3322-1` (PVC-coated protective clothing) was `Petroleum` → `Safety & PPE`

  These are recorded as manual overrides in
  `category_rules.py::_CORRECTED_CATEGORIES`, applied unconditionally
  (unlike the normal classifier, which only fires on `Uncategorized`
  rows). Worth flagging: **this was a spot-check of the 45 demo-spine
  titles, not an audit of all 287** — the same failure mode (a
  non-Uncategorized but wrong category) could exist elsewhere in the
  corpus and wouldn't be caught by anything in this pipeline.

## 6. review_severity (replaces the old always-true `needs_review`)

**Round 3 correction:** the round-2 `blocker` rule tested
`scope_source == 'title_fallback'`, which let a *scraped-but-nearly-empty*
scope through as if it were fine — 13 rows extracted real text under the
15-word usability bar and were silently counted as clean. Fixed by adding
a `scope_usable` boolean (`true` only when `scope_source == 'scraped'` AND
word count ≥ 15) and blocking on `not scope_usable` instead. This changed
the whole-corpus blocker count from 49 to **62**, and the demo-spine
blocker count from 6 to **8** — both numbers below are post-fix and are
what `validate_dataset.py` now reports.

| Severity | Meaning | Whole corpus | Demo spine |
|---|---|---|---|
| `blocker` | not usable in a demo result: `scope_usable == false`, or `Uncategorized`, or missing year | 62/287 | 8/45 |
| `minor` | usable but incomplete: certification not determined, missing amendment count, or missing source_url | 222/287 | 34/45 |
| `none` | clears all of the above | 3/287 | 3/45 |

`scope_usable` itself: **227/287 whole corpus, 37/45 demo spine** — this
is the number the retrieval pipeline should filter on, not raw
`scope_source == 'scraped'` (240/287), which still includes those 13 thin
extractions.

The 8 demo-spine blockers (the ones that would actually break a demo, if
whoever builds the query set happens to hit them):

| Standard | Why it's a blocker |
|---|---|
| IS 383:2016 | scrape found a scan but the extracted scope was only 11 words |
| IS 302-2-3 | scrape found no usable scope (falls back to title) — the one certified item in this list |
| IS 3771:2019 | scope extracted but too short (13 words) |
| IS 13580 | scope extraction failed, falls back to title |
| IS 4215 | scope extraction failed, falls back to title (4 words) |
| IS 13450-2-4 | scope extraction failed, falls back to title |
| *(2 more — filter `bis_standards_clean.csv` on the demo-spine set + `review_severity == 'blocker'` for the current exact list)* | newly caught by the `scope_usable` fix; not individually itemized here since the set can shift as scrapes are re-run |

Only 3 demo-spine rows are fully clean (`none`): the two-wheeler-helmet
and safety-helmet certification rows plus one other — everything else has
at least one open item, mostly "certification not determined," which is
the correct, honest state rather than a defect.

**Unresolved discrepancy — flagged, not silently adjusted:** the round-3
brief stated the shipped CSV had "8 blockers across 46 spine rows (one IS
number resolves to two part-rows)." The 8-blocker figure now matches
exactly after the fix above. The 46-row figure does not: this report's
demo spine is built by matching each primary standard's *exact*
`(is_number, part)` pair against the clean dataset (45 rows, one-to-one
against the 45 primaries) — deliberately stricter than matching on
`is_number` alone, which would pull in unrelated sibling parts (e.g. IS
302's Clocks and Massage-appliances rows alongside the Electric-Irons row
that's actually the primary) and inflates the spine to **49**, not 46.
Neither the strict (45) nor loose (49) join reproduces "46" exactly, and
the specific "one IS number, two part-rows" case named in the brief isn't
reproducible from the current `allied_standards_mapping_normalized.json`
+ `bis_standards_dataset.csv`. Recommend whoever wrote that number
double-check it against a fresh join rather than this report guessing at
a third join strategy to hit it.

## 7. Certification tri-state fix (round 2's highest-priority item)

Round 1 defaulted every unassessed row to `certification_type: "None"` /
`mandatory: false`. Since the (not-yet-built) frontend's
`normalizeCertificationBadge()` was specified to coerce null/empty to
`'None'`, this meant 279 standards nobody had evaluated would have
rendered identically to "confirmed, no certification needed" — in a
procurement tool, that's a false "you're clear" on products nobody
checked.

Fixed in the dataset:
- `certification_type` default is now `"Not determined"`, never `"None"`.
  `"None"` doesn't appear anywhere in the current output — asserting it
  requires an actual basis for "outside any QCO," which this pass never
  established for any row.
- `mandatory` is `true` (10 rows), or `null` (277 rows) — never `false`.
  `validate_dataset.py` asserts `mandatory` is never `false` on a row
  whose `certification_source` is `not_assessed`.
- Added `certification_source`: `qco_gazette` (10 rows) or `not_assessed`
  (277 rows). `crs_product_list` and `public_knowledge` are defined in the
  enum but unused this round — nothing in the demo spine needed them.

**Round 2 left the frontend fix undone** because no frontend scaffold
existed in the repository yet. **Round 3 implemented it**, once
`frontend/indian-standards-frontend/` appeared in the working directory —
see §12.

## 8. `latest_version` derivation

`latest_version` was null on all 287 rows going into this round, despite
209 of the original 226 rows already having a `year_published` — the standard's own
`IS <number>:<year>` naming convention makes this derivable without
scraping. Implemented in `clean.py::_resolve_latest_version`:
canonical form, including part where applicable (e.g. `IS 1489-1-2:1991`,
`IS 302-2-3:2007`), tagged `version_source: "derived_from_year"` so it's
never mistaken for a scraped revision record. Populated on 280/287 rows
(all 45 demo-spine rows); the 7 rows without a year stay null and are
`review_severity: blocker`.

This states the edition on record, nothing more — it is not a claim that
a standard hasn't been superseded since. That currency check (is this
still the latest revision?) is out of scope for this pass entirely — not
attempted, not partially done.

## 9. Reprioritized scrape queue + logging fix

The archive.org scraper was reordered to walk tier 0 (45 primary
standards) → tier 1 (61 ingested-allied standards) → tier 2 (everything
else), instead of dataset order, since the demo only ever touches tiers 0
and 1. The already-running job was stopped and restarted against the same
on-disk cache (`data/cache/archive_org/`, `data/cache/scope_results.json`)
— nothing already fetched was re-requested. `scrape_log.txt` was 0 bytes
because `print()` wasn't flushed under output redirection; every print
call now passes `flush=True`.

## 10. Round 3: frontend wired to the real data contract

The frontend project (`frontend/indian-standards-frontend/`) was built
against `src/services/mockData.ts`, which carried three fields the
pipeline has never produced (`gem_relevance`, `issuing_authority`,
`sectional_committee`) and defaulted every unassessed certification to a
false-negative `'None'`. Reconciled in favor of the real data:

- **Type contract** (`src/types/standards.ts`): removed the three
  never-populated fields; added `scope_source`, `version_source`,
  `certification_source`, `qco_reference`, `confidence`,
  `amendment_count`; `mandatory` is now `boolean | null` (tri-state, never
  defaulted to `false`); `CertificationType` gained `'Not determined'`.
- **`normalizeCertificationBadge()`** (`src/services/api.ts`): null,
  undefined, empty, or unrecognised → `'Not determined'`; only the literal
  string `"none"` (case-insensitive) → `'None'`. Verified directly:
  `normalizeCertificationBadge(null)` → `'Not determined'`,
  `normalizeCertificationBadge('none')` → `'None'`.
- **`StandardCard.tsx`**: `'Not determined'` renders as a dashed
  amber/neutral badge, visually distinct from both the black "certified"
  badge and the grey "voluntary/None" badge — not hidden, not styled as a
  pass. Removed three fabricated defaults: the hardcoded issuing-authority
  string, the constructed `standardsbis.bsbedge.com` URL guess (the
  portal doesn't expose per-standard scope pages anyway — see §3 — so a
  constructed link there was doubly wrong), and the `gem_relevance`
  pill. `source_url` now renders a link only when the dataset actually
  supplied one (real archive.org identifiers).
- **Provenance strip**: each card now shows, compactly and below the
  main content, whether scope was scraped or is a title fallback,
  that `latest_version` is a derived edition (not a currency check), and
  the certification basis with `qco_reference` inline where present.
- **Demo fallback**: `useDemoFallback` now defaults to `false`.
  `fetchRecommendations()` returns `{ standards, isDemoFallback }` instead
  of a bare array, so the UI can tell live output from the local mock
  dataset. When fallback fires, `ResultsList` renders a persistent amber
  banner ("Demo data — backend unreachable...") for as long as those
  results are shown — not a toast, doesn't disappear. The toggle stays in
  `SettingsModal.tsx`, recopied to say plainly what it does instead of
  the previous "verified... with complete source citations" framing,
  which was untrue of hardcoded mock data.
- **Landing-page statistics** (`App.tsx`): the "22K+" tile read as
  ManakMitra's own coverage; now shows **287 / 22K+** — "Indexed by
  ManakMitra" of "the full BIS catalogue" — both numbers, not one
  presented as the other. The "431 labs" figure is *not* fabricated: it's
  the real row count of `src/data/bisLabsData.ts`, each with a working
  `scopeUrl` into `lims.bis.gov.in`. Added an explicit "Source: BIS LIMS
  lab directory" citation next to its most prominent appearances (landing
  stat tile, map section header, labs nav tab tooltip) rather than
  reframing it as two numbers, since it isn't a mismatched-scale claim
  the way the standards count was.
- `mockData.ts` was not deleted — the three removed fields were stripped
  from its records and it still typechecks against the revised interface.

`npx tsc --noEmit` and `npm run build` both pass clean after these
changes.

## 12. What still needs human verification before a demo

In priority order:

1. **The demo-spine standards with `certification_type: "Not determined"`** (36 of 45 — filter `bis_standards_clean.csv` on the demo-spine set). If the demo narrative claims ManakMitra "tells you the certification requirement," these need real QCO/CRS/Hallmarking lookups before anyone relies on them live — that's the entire point of not defaulting them to a false negative.
2. **The 8 demo-spine `blocker` rows** (§6) — thin or missing scope text. Worth a manual scope write-up (marked `scope_source: drafted`, `confidence: low`, per the original project rule) if these are likely to come up in a live query.
3. **The 45-vs-46 demo-spine count discrepancy** (§6) — flagged, not resolved. Worth a second pair of eyes on the join logic before it's cited as a hard number anywhere external.
4. **IS 2925's certification scope** — confirmed mandatory only for mining use (DGMS circular), not shown to be a general-market QCO. If the demo shows this as a blanket "BIS Product Certification required," that overstates what's actually verified.
5. **IS 1786 and IS 4151's QCO citations** — order name/number/date are corroborated across multiple independent sources but not read directly off the primary gazette schedule (medium confidence, not high). Worth a direct gazette pull if this becomes a headline demo claim.
6. **A full audit of category correctness beyond the 45 demo-spine titles** — §5's four fixes were found by spot-checking the spine, not by systematically checking all 287 rows against their scraped titles. The same "wrong but not Uncategorized" failure mode could exist elsewhere.
7. **`data/cleaned_1/`** — stale round-1 output directory, safe to delete once confirmed unneeded.
8. **Visual/manual QA of the round-3 frontend changes** — the "Not determined" badge, the demo-data banner, and the provenance strip were built and typecheck/build clean, but haven't been eyeballed in a running browser session. Run `npm run dev` and click through a query before a live demo.
